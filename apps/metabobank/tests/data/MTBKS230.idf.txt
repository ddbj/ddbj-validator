Study Title	A mass spectrometric method for quantifying methionine related metabolites in hemolymph of Canton-S flies
Study Description	Female flies (Drosophila melanogaster) of Canton-S were fed with normal yeast based diet. Then hemolymph metabolites were extracted to analyze the level of methionine and methionine sulfoxide.

Protocol Description	"To allow for synchronized development with constant density, embryos of Canton-S flies were collected on agar plates (2.3% agar, 1% sucrose, and 0.35% acetic acid) with live yeast paste and directly added to the standard diet in a bottle with a fixed volume. Standard diet contained 4% cornmeal, 4% yeast, 6% glucose, and 0.8% agar with 0.3% propionic acid and 0.15% nipagin. Normally, 150-200 adult flies/bottle were obtained. Adult flies that eclosed within one day were collected and maintained for an additional two days for maturation and mating on the standard diet under conditions of 25 °C and 60-65% humidity with 12 h/12 h light/dark cycles. Flies were sorted by sex and maintained until day4.
A glass capillary was used to prick the side of the female abdomen, after which 10 flies were collected in a 0.5 mL tube that had been pierced at the bottom by a syringe. The resulting sample was then placed on top of a 1.5 mL tube and centrifuged at 4 °C and 8,000 × g for 5 minutes. Next, the collected hemolymph sample in the 1.5 mL tube was mixed and vortexed with 115 μL of 50% methanol containing 10 μM internal standards (methionine sulfone and 2-morpholinoethanesulfonic acid)."	Chloroform (250 μL) was added to the sample and vortexed. After centrifugation at 4 °C and 2,300 × g for 5 minutes, 200 μL of the supernatant was deproteinized by mixing it with 100 μL of acetonitrile.  The supernatant was placed into a pre-washed 10 kDa centrifugal device (Pall, OD010C35), and the flow-through after centrifugation at 4 °C and 14,000 × g for 5 minutes was evaporated completely using a centrifugal concentrator (TOMY, CC-105). The dried and concentrated samples were resolubilised in 100 μL of ultrapure water.	LC-MS/MS analysis was performed with LC-2040C Plus (Shimadzu). Sample (3μL) was injected using an autosampler, and molecules were separated using a PFPP column (Discovery HS F5 (2.1 mm × 150 mm, 3 μm), Sigma-Aldrich)  in a column oven at 40 °C. A gradient from solvent A (0.1% formic acid, water) to solvent B (0.1% formic acid, acetonitrile) was performed for 20 minutes.	Samples were analysed by LCMS-8050 (Shimadzu) using triple quadrupole mass spectrometer equipped with an electrospray source in both positive and negative ionisation mode. Multiple reaction monitoring method parameters were optimised by the injection and analysis of pure standards through peak integration and parameter optimisation with the use of software (Labsolutions Ver.5.109, Shimadzu). Other instrument parameters included: nebulizing gas flow: 3.0 L/min, drying gus flow: 10.0 L/min, heating gus flow: 10.0 L/min, interface temperature: 300°C, desolvation temperature: 526°C, desolvation line temperature: 250°C, heat block temperature: 400°C. Data acquisition and processing used software Labsolutions Ver.5.109.	Labsolutions (Simadzu, Ver.5.109) was used for data acquisition and processing. The sample concentration was divided by the corresponding methionine sulfone concentration and body weight fo flies for relative quantification.	Optimized multiple reaction monitoringmethods based on Primary Metabolites package ver.2 (Shimadzu) was used for the identification and quantification of each metabolite. Standard solutions of each metabolite was used as standard analytes to identify retention time.
